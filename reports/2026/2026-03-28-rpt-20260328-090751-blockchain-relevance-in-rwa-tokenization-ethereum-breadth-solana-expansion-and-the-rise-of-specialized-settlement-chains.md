## Executive Summary

The March 2026 RWA tokenization landscape shows a clear structural contrast across blockchains rather than a single dominant model. Ethereum remains the broadest institutional base layer, appearing across regulated funds, tokenized commodities, stablecoin vaults, and yield-linked products, while Base increasingly extends that footprint through lower-friction institutional fund and structured-note deployments. Solana stands out as the strongest high-growth alternative, combining dense tokenized-equity expansion with enterprise distribution and niche asset tokenization. In parallel, XRP Ledger, Avalanche, Bitcoin-native stacks, and a wider group of payment- and compliance-oriented networks are relevant through narrower but distinct mechanisms such as cross-border settlement, real estate tokenization, native Bitcoin issuance rails, and regulated stablecoin infrastructure. The key market comparison is therefore breadth versus specialization: Ethereum-led ecosystems support the widest product diversity, while other chains gain relevance through more concentrated roles in payments, settlement, compliance, or asset-specific tokenization.

## Main Analytical Sections

### Ethereum Leads on Breadth Across Institutional RWA, Yield, and Financial Product Infrastructure

**Signal:** Ethereum shows the broadest relevance in the period because it supports multiple institutional RWA mechanisms at once rather than a single narrow use case. Compared with other chains, its role is defined by product breadth across tokenized funds, precious metals, stablecoin-based financial infrastructure, and yield-linked institutional wrappers.

**Evidence:** Several developments point to Ethereum being used as the base for institution-facing tokenization and financial product design. Forum Markets positioned its Ethereum-based RWA products around legal and compliance scaling, while The Ether Machine emphasized an Ethereum-focused strategy for institutional exposure and yield generation through staking and validator infrastructure. Matrixdock launched the silver-backed XAGm token on Ethereum, and Amundi and Spiko used Ethereum smart contracts alongside Stellar for a tokenized overnight swap fund. Ethereum also appeared in layered stablecoin and yield structures through Aon’s USDC premium payment flow, Lido’s EarnUSD vault, Concrete and Theo’s thUSD Genesis Vault, and YieldNest’s ERC-4626-compliant ynRWAx vault, reinforcing Ethereum’s repeated use for composable, institution-oriented product architecture. One source set also explicitly described Ethereum as underpinning roughly 58% of RWA activity and linked NYSE and Securitize’s tokenized securities platform to Ethereum.

**Strategic Implication:** Ethereum’s relevance is less about one headline deployment than about repeated institutional reuse across different product types. That makes it the clearest comparison benchmark for general-purpose RWA infrastructure in the current scope.

**Key Facts:**
- Forum Markets and The Ether Machine both advanced Ethereum-centered institutional RWA strategies, but through different mechanisms: compliance scaling versus yield-focused exposure.
- Ethereum supported both asset-backed tokenization and fund-style structures, including XAGm and the Amundi–Spiko tokenized overnight swap fund.
- Stablecoin vaults and RWA-yield products on Ethereum reinforced its role as a composable financial infrastructure layer.

### Base Is Emerging as Ethereum’s Institutional Extension Rather Than a Separate Competitive Model

**Signal:** Base appears in the dataset as an institutional scaling layer that inherits Ethereum-oriented tokenization logic while differentiating through deployment efficiency, structured products, and fund infrastructure. Compared with Ethereum mainnet, Base’s relevance is narrower but increasingly concentrated in institutional wrappers and settlement-led product launches.

**Evidence:** Apex Group and Coinbase Asset Management launched a tokenized Bitcoin yield fund on Base with on-chain settlement of yield-bearing fund units, while GLDY used Base alongside Fireblocks and Chainlink for a tokenized gold fund structure. Omnes also used the ERC-3643 standard on Base for an institutional Bitcoin mining structured note, reinforcing Base’s positioning in regulated-style product packaging. Elsewhere, Base appeared in institutional digital infrastructure discussions through Morpho V2 and treasury-tokenization-linked lending, in OpenVPP’s migration to Base, and in a payment-liquidity context through Mezo and Aerodrome’s Bitcoin DeFi liquidity build on Base. Across these examples, Base is less broad than Ethereum but more recurrent than many alternative chains in institution-facing implementation.

**Strategic Implication:** Base’s relevance is strongest where institutions want Ethereum-compatible tokenization with a more deployment-oriented operating model. This positions it as a meaningful secondary layer in the comparison, especially for funds, structured notes, and settlement-linked financial products.

**Key Facts:**
- Base supported a tokenized Bitcoin yield fund, a tokenized gold fund structure, and an institutional Bitcoin mining structured note.
- Evidence in the period links Base both to fund issuance and to infrastructure-first deployments such as institutional lending and treasury tokenization.
- Base’s pattern is concentrated and extension-like, contrasting with Ethereum’s broader product diversity.

### Solana Combines Broadening Enterprise Access With Dense Asset-Specific Tokenization

**Signal:** Solana’s relevance comes from a different structure than Ethereum’s: not the broadest institutional base layer, but a dense cluster of tokenized equity, payment, and niche-asset launches that show rapid ecosystem expansion. Compared with Base and other alternatives, Solana appears both in enterprise onboarding and in consumer-facing or specialized tokenization formats.

**Evidence:** Solana was used by Ondo Finance to add more than 60 tokenized stocks and ETFs, and separate coverage linked the chain to record RWA active DeFi TVL of $465 million and a broader reorientation toward institutional finance. It also appeared in an enterprise platform launch involving Mastercard, Worldpay, and Western Union, with Elliptic selected as compliance partner for the Solana Developer Platform. Beyond equities and enterprise rails, the chain supported Western Union and Crossmint’s USD stablecoin effort, Plume’s Nest integration for institutional-grade RWA funds, Aon’s use of PYUSD on Solana, Tessera’s private equity tokenization platform, Royaltiz’s on-chain talent asset, Kondoo Digital’s regulated capital raise via tZERO, and Beeline’s home-equity tokenization initiative. This makes Solana one of the most diversified non-Ethereum ecosystems in the report, though its diversity is concentrated around expansionary deployment rather than Ethereum-style multi-layer composability.

**Strategic Implication:** Solana is relevant because it is accumulating both enterprise distribution channels and a thick layer of asset-specific tokenization activity. In comparison terms, it looks less like Ethereum’s default institutional base and more like the fastest-growing alternative platform for scaled issuance and distribution.

**Key Facts:**
- Solana combined enterprise platform expansion with large-scale tokenized equities growth through Ondo’s addition of 60+ stocks and ETFs.
- The chain also appeared in specialized deployments spanning private equity, talent assets, home equity, regulated capital raising, and institutional-grade RWA funds.
- Compliance infrastructure was embedded directly through Elliptic’s role in the Solana Developer Platform.

### XRP Ledger and Payment-Led Networks Gain Relevance Through Regulated Settlement and Cross-Border Trade Flows

**Signal:** A separate comparison tier is formed by XRP Ledger and other payment- and compliance-oriented networks whose relevance is concentrated in settlement, trade flows, and regulated cross-border movement rather than broad product issuance. Compared with Ethereum and Solana, these chains are narrower, but their mechanisms are more specialized.

**Evidence:** XRP Ledger appeared repeatedly in settlement and tokenized trade contexts: Ripple’s acquisition of BC Payments Australia supported its Australian license push; RLUSD and AUDD were referenced for XRP Ledger-based settlement activity; Ripple joined Singapore’s BLOOM trade settlement pilot; and XRPL was linked to the ECB’s move to accept DLT-issued tokenized securities as collateral through an XRP Ledger-based platform. BTCC Exchange’s listing of Real Token and YPF Luz’s ENERTOKEN launch on XRPL extended this pattern into traditional-asset integration and energy tokenization, while another March report highlighted an AI-driven XRPL security push for payments and tokenization growth. Adjacent networks reinforced the same mechanism from different angles: Hedera integrated USDT0 for cross-chain stablecoin liquidity, Tempo combined payments-first blockchain design with Elliptic compliance coverage, Canton appeared through ClearToken’s regulated FX tokenization and Mesh’s connection of digital assets to traditional capital markets, and Stellar combined RedStone oracle infrastructure with Bitget Wallet’s stablecoin payment expansion.

**Strategic Implication:** These blockchains matter less for broad issuance diversity and more for infrastructure roles where compliance, fast settlement, and regulated payment flows are central. In the current comparison, they form the strongest specialist counterweight to Ethereum- and Solana-led general tokenization ecosystems.

**Key Facts:**
- XRP Ledger’s relevance in March 2026 was repeatedly tied to trade settlement, collateral use, cross-border payments, and large-scale tokenization examples such as ENERTOKEN.
- Hedera, Tempo, Canton, and Stellar each reinforced payment or regulated-settlement functions through distinct infrastructure layers.
- The contrast with Ethereum and Solana is structural: these chains are more focused on transaction and compliance rails than on broad product variety.

### Bitcoin and Avalanche Show High Relevance Through Distinct Institutional-Native Mechanisms Rather Than Broad Ecosystem Breadth

**Signal:** Bitcoin and Avalanche are relevant in the dataset through concentrated, chain-specific institutional mechanisms. Compared with Ethereum’s breadth or Solana’s expansionary mix, both ecosystems appear more focused: Bitcoin on native issuance and settlement innovations inside its own stack, and Avalanche on large-scale institutional integrations and enterprise payment finality.

**Evidence:** Bitcoin-related developments included HTX opening trading for Bitway, a Bitcoin-native Layer 1 aimed at gasless transactions, BTC-collateralized lending, and direct RWA tokenization without EVM bridges; Utexo’s native USDT settlement on Bitcoin and Lightning; OP_NET’s native DeFi stack on Bitcoin Layer 1; and STOKR’s effort to strengthen tokenized asset issuance on Liquid Network. These examples split between Bitcoin-native payments, DeFi, and issuance rails. Avalanche, by contrast, appeared in a five-year integration project tied to more than $200 billion in real estate assets, in Grayscale’s Avalanche Staking ETF, and through Ava Labs’ participation in Mastercard’s Crypto Partner Program for cross-border stablecoin settlement. The result is a direct comparison between Bitcoin’s internal stack expansion and Avalanche’s institution-led deployments around real estate, staking access, and payment finality.

**Strategic Implication:** Both chains are relevant, but through concentrated roles rather than ecosystem-wide diversity. Bitcoin’s significance lies in making its own base and layer-2 environment operational for RWA and stablecoin activity, while Avalanche’s lies in a smaller number of large institutional implementations.

**Key Facts:**
- Bitcoin-related relevance in the period centered on native infrastructure: Bitway, Utexo, OP_NET, and Liquid-based issuance via STOKR.
- Avalanche’s relevance was anchored by large institutional projects, including a major real estate tokenization effort and a staking ETF.
- The comparison highlights two different concentration patterns: Bitcoin’s internal infrastructure build-out versus Avalanche’s enterprise-scale integrations.

### Interoperability and Custom-Chain Strategies Form a Secondary but Distinct Relevance Layer

**Signal:** Beyond the major public-chain comparison, a secondary relevance layer is emerging around interoperability-first designs and bespoke blockchain environments built for specific institutional workflows. Compared with broad public-chain ecosystems, these initiatives prioritize controlled connectivity, chain-to-chain settlement, or custom operating environments.

**Evidence:** KoreInside launched interoperability for public blockchains including Ethereum, Solana, and Coinbase environments to support RWA enablement, while Base58 Labs presented a multi-chain yield infrastructure spanning BTC, ETH, SOL, and PAXG. xChange also offered a unified execution layer across Ethereum and Solana for atomic trading of tokenized equities. Pharos added another interoperability-focused model by integrating USDC and CCTP on its mainnet to support settlement across more than 20 blockchains and over 400 transaction pathways without third-party bridges. Separate proprietary-chain efforts pointed in the same direction from a different angle: Bitpanda’s Vision Chain, Monument Bank’s deposit tokenization on Midnight, Startale’s Strium Layer-1, and BitGo’s work with ZKSync on tokenized deposit infrastructure all showed institutions exploring dedicated environments instead of relying only on major general-purpose chains.

**Strategic Implication:** This layer suggests that blockchain relevance is not only determined by standalone chain adoption, but also by how effectively a network connects assets, workflows, and regulated institutions across environments. In comparison terms, interoperability and custom-chain design remain secondary to the major ecosystems, but they are becoming a meaningful route to institutional fit.

**Key Facts:**
- Interoperability appeared through KoreInside, Base58 Labs, xChange, and Pharos, each addressing cross-chain access or settlement in different ways.
- Custom-chain and proprietary infrastructure examples included Vision Chain, Midnight, Strium, and BitGo’s tokenized deposit work with ZKSync.
- These models differ from Ethereum- and Solana-style ecosystem breadth by emphasizing controlled integration and workflow-specific design.

### Peripheral but Notable Chains Show Relevance Through Highly Specific Functional Niches

**Signal:** A final comparison layer consists of chains whose relevance is visible through highly specific functional niches rather than recurring cross-market usage. These platforms are materially present in the dataset, but their activity is more isolated and mechanism-specific.

**Evidence:** Polygon appeared in KfW and DZ BANK’s crypto security issuance through a Smart Bond Contract and in Revolut’s on-chain stablecoin transaction activity, emphasizing low-cost high-throughput finance. XDC was described through enterprise tokenized debt and structured credit use cases. BNB Chain’s relevance came through the listing of Tether’s XAUT tokenized gold, contrasting with multi-chain equity-style deployment. TRON entered the picture both via Mastercard’s Crypto Partner Program and Anchorage Digital’s regulated custody integration. Other specialized single-mechanism examples included Rayls with USDXP, Monad with EURW, ICP through Solum Global’s healthcare stablecoin payment platform, Sonic’s USSD stablecoin, Hyperliquid’s S&P 500 perpetual contract, and Tempo’s machine-payments-oriented blockchain.

**Strategic Implication:** These chains should be read as niche specialists within the comparison set. Their relevance is real but concentrated, and they do not yet show the same breadth or repeated institutional range visible on Ethereum, Solana, Base, or payment-led settlement networks such as XRPL.

**Key Facts:**
- Polygon and XDC were tied to low-cost issuance and enterprise structured-finance activity.
- BNB Chain, TRON, ICP, Rayls, Monad, Sonic, Hyperliquid, and Tempo each appeared through narrow but distinct functional roles.
- Their March relevance is best understood as specialization rather than broad ecosystem positioning.

## Cross-Cutting Observations
