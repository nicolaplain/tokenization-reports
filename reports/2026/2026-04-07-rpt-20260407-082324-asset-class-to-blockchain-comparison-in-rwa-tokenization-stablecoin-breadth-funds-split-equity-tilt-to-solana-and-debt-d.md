## Executive Summary

Stablecoin is the dominant asset class in scope and also the broadest from a blockchain-counterpart perspective, with the evidence showing deployment across major general-purpose networks, specialized settlement chains, and payment-focused infrastructures rather than a single-chain model. Within that broad stablecoin field, Ethereum and Solana remain the most visible counterparts, while XRP Ledger, TRON, and Base show clearer specialization patterns. Funds and equity present a more concentrated comparison: funds are led by Ethereum and Solana but extend into specialized networks such as Avalanche, Stellar, Canton, Base, and XRP Ledger, while equity activity tilts more strongly toward Solana even as Ethereum remains a major parallel venue and several alternative chains support niche issuance and market-structure functions. Debt is the smallest in-scope asset class but shows the most infrastructure diversity relative to its size, with meaningful activity on Ethereum, Solana, Avalanche, Canton, Polygon, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Aptos, and XDC, indicating that fixed-income and credit tokenization is being matched to highly specific settlement, governance, and compliance requirements.

### Stablecoin shows the broadest blockchain distribution, with Ethereum and Solana leading but not containing the market

**Signal:** Stablecoin is the most structurally broad asset class in the selected scope, and its blockchain relationships are more dispersed than those of funds, equity, or debt. Ethereum and Solana are the leading counterparts, but the evidence shows stablecoin deployment spreading across specialized, payment-oriented, and compliance-tailored chains.

**Evidence:** Ethereum appears repeatedly in stablecoin infrastructure tied to settlement, yield, and regulated issuance, including AllUnity’s CHFAU launch on Ethereum, Lido’s EarnUSD vault, and Circle’s Ethereum/Arc-linked stablecoin utility updates. Solana also appears repeatedly through institution-facing or payments-linked deployments, including OSL Group’s USDGO launch, Western Union’s stablecoin with Crossmint, and broader reporting around Solana-based stablecoin initiatives. Beyond those two, stablecoin activity extends across XRP Ledger through Société Générale’s EURCV expansion and Ripple’s RLUSD-related infrastructure, across Polygon through Toku’s payroll rails and Circle’s native USDC settlement for Polymarket, and across additional networks including Avalanche, Celo, Cardano, XDC, Stellar, Hedera, Sui, Bitcoin, Monad, Plasma, Sonic, Tempo, Arc, and Rayls. This combination supports the structural pattern that stablecoin deployment is increasingly matched to operational priorities such as settlement speed, payment integration, compliance tooling, confidentiality, or cross-chain conversion.

**Strategic Implication:** Stablecoin is not consolidating around a single blockchain operating model. Instead, it is becoming the asset class where blockchain selection is most function-specific, with dominant chains retaining scale while specialized chains win around particular settlement, compliance, or payment niches.
