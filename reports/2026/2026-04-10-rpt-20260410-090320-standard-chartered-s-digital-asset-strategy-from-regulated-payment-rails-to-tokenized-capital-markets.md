## Executive Summary

Standard Chartered’s activity across the period is best understood as a regulated, infrastructure-led digital asset strategy rather than a single-product push. The strongest evidence shows the bank building across payment rails, custody and institutional access, and tokenized capital-markets infrastructure at the same time: pursuing stablecoin licensing in Hong Kong, launching tokenized deposit rails with Ant International, expanding institutional custody and trading partnerships with firms such as OKX and Coinbase, and supporting tokenized bond and treasury-linked structures through initiatives including Libeara. Alongside these operating moves, the bank’s research and leadership signals indicate a deliberate effort to differentiate where tokenization adds measurable value, especially in non-stablecoin RWAs and treasury-management use cases, while maintaining a compliance-first posture.

### Regulated Digital Money and Tokenized Deposit Rails Form the Core of Standard Chartered’s Operating Model

**Signal:** Standard Chartered’s clearest structural pattern is the build-out of regulated on-chain money infrastructure spanning stablecoin pathways and tokenized bank-deposit rails. Rather than relying on a single format, the bank appears to be positioning across multiple regulated payment mechanisms.

**Evidence:** In Hong Kong, Standard Chartered’s joint venture Anchorpoint applied for a stablecoin licence, and later reporting indicated the bank was set to receive one of the first stablecoin issuer licences under that framework. In parallel, Standard Chartered launched blockchain-based tokenized deposits in SGD and USD with Ant International to support real-time, 24/7 cash management and liquidity movement. Additional reporting tied the bank to broader tokenized-deposit discussions and to payment-oriented product innovation such as the DeCard stablecoin credit card pilot, which enabled purchases settled in stablecoins including USDC and USDT. Leadership changes also reinforced this direction, with the appointment of Naveen Mallela as Global Head of Payments and subsequent market commentary linking the bank’s payments outlook to stablecoin adoption.

**Strategic Implication:** This pattern suggests Standard Chartered is treating regulated digital money as foundational infrastructure for broader tokenization activity. By covering both stablecoin and tokenized-deposit models, the bank is positioning to serve different payment, treasury, and settlement use cases within regulated frameworks.

**Key Facts:**
- Anchorpoint, a Standard Chartered-led joint venture, applied for a Hong Kong stablecoin licence.
- Standard Chartered and Ant International launched tokenized deposits in SGD and USD for round-the-clock treasury and cash-management use cases.
- Standard Chartered was also linked to the DeCard stablecoin credit card pilot and appointed a new global payments lead with blockchain-payments experience.
