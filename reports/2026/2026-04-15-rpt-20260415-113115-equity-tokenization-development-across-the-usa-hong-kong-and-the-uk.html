Equity Tokenization Development Across the USA, Hong Kong, and the UK

Executive Summary

Equity tokenization in the selected scope is led by the USA both in volume and in the breadth of mechanism-level development, with repeated evidence of regulated issuance, exchange-style trading venues, data and settlement infrastructure, and proposals to extend tokenized equities into mainstream market access. By contrast, Hong Kong and the UK appear as smaller but distinct secondary centers: Hong Kong is associated more with compliant access routes, stablecoin-enabled trading, and cross-border distribution linkages, while the UK is more closely tied to governance-heavy issuance models and interoperability infrastructure connecting tokenized and conventional market workflows. The comparison shows a structurally uneven market in which the USA is building a fuller equity tokenization stack, while Hong Kong and the UK are advancing narrower, more specialized pathways.

USA: Compliance-Centric Equity Models Are Expanding Faster Than Higher-Risk Formats

Signal: Within the U.S.-led segment, the dominant mechanism is not tokenization alone but tokenization embedded in established compliance and disclosure structures. This stands in contrast to peripheral models that offer synthetic or rights-limited equity exposure without direct shareholder ownership.

Evidence: Multiple U.S. developments emphasized investor-rights continuity and formal regulatory alignment. Figure’s offering was SEC-registered; Securitize tied tokenized Currenc Group shares to SEC-regulated frameworks; and Nasdaq’s public commentary stressed that tokenized securities can preserve traditional shareholder rights. Additional regulatory framing came from U.S. banking agencies stating that capital treatment for tokenized and standard securities should be the same, the SEC Investor Committee calling for clearer disclosures on ownership, voting, and dividend rights, and SEC safe-harbor guidance clarifying that non-custodial wallet interfaces are not broker-dealers even for tokenized securities. By comparison, April coverage of BingX and Bitget’s SpaceX-linked pre-IPO products explicitly noted that buyers would not own actual shares or receive direct shareholder rights.

Strategic Implication: The comparison suggests that the more structurally durable equity-tokenization pathway in scope is the regulated model that preserves conventional investor protections. Synthetic pre-IPO exposure is present, but the evidence positions it as a more speculative edge case rather than the market’s core institutional direction.
