## Executive Summary

Issuance-platform activity in early 2026 was led by two structurally important patterns: first, institutional platforms embedding token issuance into regulated banking, treasury, settlement, and capital-markets infrastructure; second, dedicated tokenized-equity platforms scaling through multi-chain distribution, exchange integration, and 1:1-backed issuance models. Around that core, issuance platforms also diversified into regulated fund, bond, real estate, and niche-asset workflows, with a clear split between end-to-end institutional infrastructure, bank-led controlled environments, and highly specialized platforms tailored to particular asset classes such as property, transport, commodities, IP, reinsurance, and trade finance. The overall market shape is not a single tokenization trend but a layered platform landscape: incumbent-led regulated infrastructure at the center, scalable equity issuance rails as a major secondary growth area, and specialized issuance stacks expanding the addressable asset universe.

## Main Analytical Sections

### Institutional issuance platforms are becoming embedded financial infrastructure

**Signal:** The strongest structural signal is the rise of issuance platforms that are no longer positioned as standalone tokenization tools, but as embedded components of regulated banking, treasury, settlement, and post-trade infrastructure. These platforms are being used to issue tokenized deposits, stablecoins, money market products, digital bonds, and other financial instruments within controlled institutional environments.

**Evidence:** Multiple initiatives show incumbents building issuance directly into legacy financial workflows. BNY expanded its Digital Assets platform to support on-chain mirrored client deposits and a stablecoin reserves fund, while DZ Bank secured a MiCAR license and launched meinKrypto for digital asset trading and stablecoin initiatives. State Street launched a digital asset platform, UBS considered crypto trading through its private bank, and ClearBank selected Taurus PROTECT to support stablecoin and tokenized-asset services. Canton-related developments reinforced this pattern: JPMorgan moved JPM issuance to Canton, LSEG’s tokenized deposit solution was used for on-chain repo activity, Visa joined Canton as a Super Validator, and HSBC later completed a tokenized deposit pilot on Canton with atomic settlement. Enterprise treasury integration also advanced through BitGo Mint and Ripple Treasury, both of which positioned issuance and redemption within institutional treasury operations.

**Strategic Implication:** Issuance platforms are increasingly being treated as core financial infrastructure rather than experimental wrappers around blockchain assets. This raises the importance of compliance, interoperability, treasury integration, and settlement design as primary differentiators in the next phase of tokenized markets.

**Key Facts:**
- BNY, DZ Bank, State Street, UBS, ClearBank, JPMorgan, HSBC, Visa, BitGo, and Ripple all appeared in platform developments tied to regulated issuance or treasury infrastructure.
- Canton repeatedly appeared as a platform layer for tokenized deposits, stablecoin-related payments, repo activity, and cross-chain institutional asset movement.
- Several developments combined issuance with custody, treasury controls, or post-trade settlement rather than focusing only on primary token creation.

### Tokenized-equity issuance platforms are scaling through dedicated rails, exchange distribution, and multi-chain reach

**Signal:** A second major structural pattern is the emergence of tokenized-equity issuance platforms as distinct market rails, with platforms competing on listing breadth, 1:1 collateral models, settlement mechanics, and exchange connectivity. The leading examples are not generic RWA stacks; they are specialized equity issuance and trading infrastructures.

**Evidence:** Figure’s Onchain Public Equity Network enabled a blockchain-native common equity offering and the launch of tokenized stock FGRD with T+0 settlement and programmable compliance. xStocks scaled from dozens of listings to 100 tokenized equities, operated across Ethereum and Solana, reported large cumulative transaction volume, and extended distribution through Kraken, Bybit, and related incentive programs. Ondo Finance expanded tokenized stocks and ETFs across Ethereum, Solana, and BNB Chain, with 250-plus tokenized securities and additional listings including BlackRock’s IBIT. Other platforms broadened the field through different architectures: Binance partnered with Ondo to relaunch tokenized stock trading, Capital B used Stokr on Liquid, ST0x launched with European prospectus approval, and Galaxy Digital partnered with Superstate on Solana to tokenize public equities.

**Strategic Implication:** Tokenized equities are moving from isolated product launches toward platform competition based on liquidity design, chain strategy, and integration with trading venues. This suggests that issuance success in equities increasingly depends on distribution and settlement architecture, not only on the tokenized wrapper itself.

**Key Facts:**
- Figure, xStocks, Ondo, Binance, Stokr, ST0x, and Galaxy Digital all appeared in equity-oriented issuance platform developments.
- Multi-chain positioning was a recurring differentiator, especially across Ethereum, Solana, BNB Chain, and Liquid.
- Repeated evidence emphasized 1:1 backing, continuous trading, atomic or immediate settlement, and integration with exchange ecosystems.

### Regulated securities and fund issuance platforms are converging around compliance-led digital wrappers

**Signal:** A parallel institutional segment is the use of issuance platforms for regulated securities, funds, and structured products, where the differentiating mechanism is compliance sequencing rather than retail-scale distribution. These platforms emphasize registration, governance, standards-based token formats, and regulated fund or note issuance.

**Evidence:** Securitize appeared repeatedly in compliance-led issuance, first through a public filing tied to its tokenized securities strategy and later through tokenizing Currenc Group’s ordinary shares for on-chain trading on Ethereum and Solana. UK Financial advanced multiple ERC-3643-based pathways, including MayaFund’s planned upgrade, SMPRA governance steps, and a later asset-backed ERC-3643 security token with on-chain compliance verification. BNP Paribas Asset Management piloted tokenization of a French money market fund on AssetFoundry, while Legal & General Asset Management tokenized MMFs through Calastone’s Tokenised Distribution Network across multiple currencies. Citi issued a digitally native structured note on Euroclear’s D-FMI DLT, and Nasdaq’s Seturion work with Boerse Stuttgart targeted tokenized securities settlement in Europe.

**Strategic Implication:** This segment shows issuance platforms evolving into regulated securities infrastructure with a strong focus on legal form, transfer controls, and compatible post-trade processes. The market is not only expanding into new assets; it is also standardizing how regulated instruments are digitally issued and distributed.

**Key Facts:**
- Securitize, UK Financial, BNP Paribas Asset Management, Legal & General Asset Management, Citi, Euroclear, Nasdaq, and Boerse Stuttgart were tied to compliance-led issuance pathways.
- ERC-3643, public filing, governance votes, and tokenised distribution networks were recurring mechanisms in this segment.
- Fund tokenization and securities tokenization increasingly appeared alongside settlement and distribution infrastructure rather than as isolated issuance events.

### Bond, deposit, and private-fund issuance platforms are modernizing traditional capital-market workflows

**Signal:** Within institutional issuance, a distinct mechanism-level pattern is the digitization of conventional capital-market instruments through platforms built for bond lifecycles, private-fund administration, and dematerialized issuance. These platforms focus on end-to-end workflow modernization rather than generalized token access.

**Evidence:** HSBC’s Orion platform appeared repeatedly in digital bond issuance, supporting Mirae Asset’s dual-currency digital bonds and later being selected for the UK’s digital gilt issuance. Project Samara piloted a full DLT-based bond lifecycle including issuance, bidding, coupon payment, redemption, secondary trading, and settlement with regulatory approval. LSEG and Apex worked on Digital Market Infrastructure for private funds, while Clearstream and Euroclear launched a dematerialized Eurobond issuance service. Finexity placed a hybrid bond combining conventional and tokenized tranches, and Ripple partnered with Kyobo Life Insurance on tokenized government bond settlement.

**Strategic Implication:** These cases show issuance platforms moving upstream into market structure itself, not merely asset packaging. The key platform opportunity here is operational redesign of established issuance and settlement workflows for instruments that already have institutional demand.

**Key Facts:**
- HSBC Orion, Project Samara, LSEG Digital Market Infrastructure, Clearstream-Euroclear, Finexity, and Ripple-Kyobo were all tied to traditional fixed-income or fund workflows.
- Several examples covered complete lifecycle functions including issuance, settlement, redemption, and secondary trading.
- Bond and fund tokenization repeatedly relied on regulated or approved test environments rather than open-market launch models.

### Real-estate issuance platforms remain important, but are fragmenting into distinct regulatory and operating models

**Signal:** Real-estate issuance platforms remained a major asset-specific category, but the evidence points to fragmentation across several operating models: regulated platform approval, tokenized property fractions, corporate repositioning around property tokenization, and partnership-led issuance networks.

**Evidence:** SmartCrowd’s Nawy Shares secured VARA in-principle approval for a tokenized real-estate platform, while the National Bank of Kazakhstan piloted property tokens mapped to one square meter of commercial real estate. HUMBL rebranded as TAP Real Estate and licensed TAP platform technologies including wallet and token engine tools to support real-estate issuance and management. Vardhman Realty UAE partnered with RealTokenize.io for a $300 million global real-estate tokenization initiative, Valereum signed an MOU with Integra around regulated issuance through VLRM Markets, and World Liberty Financial’s planned tokenization of loan revenue interests in a Maldives hospitality asset involved Securitize and DarGlobal. Smart Props Solution also launched a real-estate tokenization presale using an Ethereum Layer 2-based issuance model.

**Strategic Implication:** Real-estate tokenization is not consolidating around one platform template. Instead, issuance platforms in property are differentiating through regulatory pathway, asset-mapping design, and whether they focus on direct ownership, revenue interests, or broader issuance infrastructure.

**Key Facts:**
- Real-estate issuance appeared through approvals, pilots, partnerships, rebrands, and presale distribution models.
- Asset-to-token mapping and regulated issuance frameworks were central in several examples.
- Some real-estate initiatives were confirmed approvals or pilots, while others were exploratory or planned and remained proposal-stage.

### Stablecoin and tokenized-money issuance platforms are differentiating by payment rails, banking integration, and treasury tooling

**Signal:** Stablecoin-related issuance platforms formed a broad but internally segmented field, with different platforms specializing in bank issuance, payment settlement, treasury operations, or white-label deployment for financial institutions. The dominant pattern is not uniform stablecoin growth but functional specialization across the tokenized-money stack.

**Evidence:** Circle Mint supported internal treasury transfers and later expanded through CPN Managed Payments; Movantis joined Circle Payments Network, and Hecto Financial integrated with Circle’s network for cross-border settlement. Q2 Innovation Studio partnered with Stablecore to help banks and credit unions launch stablecoins and tokenized deposits, while Finzly introduced Token Galaxy to unify traditional and tokenized banking flows. MetaComp’s StableX Network, Ark Labs’ Arkade, and BitGo Mint each reflected different issuance architectures. On the bank-led side, HSBC welcomed a Hong Kong stablecoin issuer licence, and ClearBank Europe’s MiCAR CASP status reinforced compliance-led infrastructure expansion. Hong Kong’s EnsembleX model added a broader licensing and reserve-backed framework for stablecoins, tokenised deposits, and wholesale CBDC settlement.

**Strategic Implication:** The tokenized-money segment is separating into several platform layers: issuance and minting, payment-network connectivity, bank deployment tooling, and regulatory stack design. That segmentation suggests future competition will center on infrastructure fit and regulated interoperability rather than simple issuance scale.

**Key Facts:**
- Circle, Q2-Stablecore, Finzly, MetaComp, Ark Labs, BitGo, HSBC, and EnsembleX represented different platform roles within tokenized money.
- Cross-border settlement, treasury movement, white-label bank deployment, and full-reserve licensing frameworks were distinct recurring mechanisms.
- Some developments were completed launches or integrations, while others, such as certain bank stablecoin plans, were still explicitly planned.

### End-to-end issuance platforms are competing on integrated onboarding, compliance, and lifecycle management

**Signal:** Another important platform pattern is the rise of integrated issuance stacks that package onboarding, identity, compliance, issuance, and settlement into a single workflow. These platforms differentiate themselves by reducing vendor fragmentation and shortening deployment timelines.

**Evidence:** Nomyx joined Ownera’s SuperApps Platform to provide token deployment, digital identity, compliance, and primary issuance in one stack. String Metaverse launched Tokenization as a Service with real-time settlement and continuous trading. OTCM Protocol’s Issuers Gateway focused on structured onboarding for public companies tokenizing securities, while Nasdaq-Talos and Murex-Quant integrations extended tokenized issuance into risk and collateral workflows. Zama’s integration with the T-REX Ledger added confidentiality as part of institutional tokenized-asset infrastructure.

**Strategic Implication:** Integrated stacks are becoming a distinct competitive model within issuance infrastructure. As tokenization programs move from pilots to production, workflow consolidation and institutional operability appear to matter as much as the token standard or blockchain choice.

**Key Facts:**
- Several platforms positioned themselves around full-process execution rather than a single issuance function.
- Integration points included identity, confidentiality, collateral, onboarding, compliance, and settlement.
- This pattern was especially visible where platforms aimed to serve public issuers or institutional operators.

### Specialized issuance platforms continue to expand tokenization into non-traditional asset classes

**Signal:** Beyond the institutional and equity core, issuance platforms are spreading into a wide range of niche or previously illiquid asset classes, with platforms designed around the specific economics and compliance needs of each underlying asset. This expansion is broad, but structurally it is characterized by narrow specialization rather than one convergent model.

**Evidence:** Transport assets were tokenized through Mitsui’s Alterna platform, while Galaxy launched a tokenized CLO on Avalanche. Commodities and luxury assets saw several dedicated models, including Arowana’s gold platform on Arbitrum, Ripple’s diamond tokenization with XRPL and Ripple Custody, Litro’s crude oil project, and Novarra BBX/Happy Gold’s production-linked gold platform. Alternative income and rights-based assets also expanded: Eros Universe and Datavault AI focused on IP and NIL rights, Esperanza Securities tokenized live entertainment revenue streams, dClimate and Oxbridge Re targeted reinsurance, Edenex explored export finance, Oceanus and Clearwater tested trade-finance tokenization via ODIN, JP3E and TBURN targeted industrial capacity and contracts, and SimpleChain focused on freight. Other niche issuance models included Pineapple Express for convertible notes, Maluku Digital Asset with Chintai for a regulated SAFT tied to a Natural Asset Fund, and Genius Coffee’s non-financial goods token on RWALTD.

**Strategic Implication:** Specialized issuance platforms are enlarging the practical scope of tokenization, but the market remains highly asset-specific at this edge. The recurring pattern is customization: platform design is being shaped by each asset’s revenue logic, custody requirements, and regulatory constraints.

**Key Facts:**
- Niche issuance covered commodities, transport, industrial contracts, reinsurance, entertainment rights, trade finance, export financing, freight, and alternative securities.
- These platforms generally emphasized bespoke issuance logic rather than broad horizontal infrastructure.
- Several initiatives were launched or piloted, while some remained exploratory or proposal-stage and should be read accordingly.
