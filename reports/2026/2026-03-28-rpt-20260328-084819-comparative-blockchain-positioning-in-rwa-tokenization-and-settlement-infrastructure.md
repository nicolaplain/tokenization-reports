## Executive Summary

Ethereum remains the broadest and most repeatedly evidenced blockchain layer in this dataset, appearing across tokenized securities, funds, commodities, credit infrastructure, and multi-chain architectures that still anchor to Ethereum for programmability and liquidity. In contrast, several other chains show more concentrated positioning: Solana is repeatedly tied to institutional distribution, payments, and compliance-led tokenized products; XRP Ledger and Ripple-related infrastructure appear in payment, collateral, trade settlement, and sector-specific tokenization use cases; Avalanche shows a dual profile across large-scale asset migration and enterprise payment partnerships; and a wider set of specialized or alternative chains compete through narrow functional advantages such as regulated settlement, low-cost transfers, custody, cross-chain liquidity, or commodity-specific tokenization. Across the comparison set, the clearest structural divide is between broad-core infrastructure chains and narrower mechanism-specific networks, with multi-chain designs increasingly used to combine those roles rather than replace them.

## Main Analytical Sections

### Ethereum as the Broadest Core Layer, with Multi-Chain Extensions Built Around It

**Signal:** Ethereum is the most broadly positioned blockchain in the dataset, recurring across institutional tokenization, stablecoin and credit infrastructure, commodity-backed assets, and tokenized fund design. Compared with other chains that appear in narrower functional roles, Ethereum is repeatedly used as the programmable base layer even when projects adopt multi-chain distribution.

**Evidence:** For example, Ethereum appears in the NYSE–Securitize tokenized securities context and in Aave V4’s pending launch, both tied to credit and securities infrastructure. It also underpins products and issuance models such as Forum Markets’ structured RWA products, Lido’s EarnUSD vault, Matrixdock’s silver token, and BlackRock’s staked Ethereum ETF framework. In multi-chain structures, Ethereum remains a central anchor rather than a discarded layer: Techemynt deploys across Ethereum, Polygon, and Base; the Amundi–Spiko tokenized overnight swap fund uses Ethereum for programmable settlement and Chainlink-linked NAV recording while pairing it with Stellar for transfers; xStocks spans Ethereum with Solana and later broader multi-chain deployment; and Prividium is described as anchored to Ethereum via ZKsync.

**Strategic Implication:** The comparison suggests Ethereum retains the widest functional relevance in tokenized asset markets because it supports both stand-alone institutional products and hybrid architectures that extend distribution elsewhere. Other chains may win on cost, speed, or specialization, but the evidence here repeatedly places Ethereum at the center of higher-complexity RWA designs.

**Key Facts:**
- Ethereum is linked in the summaries to tokenized securities, credit infrastructure, vaults, commodities, ETFs, and tokenized funds.
- Multi-chain projects in the dataset frequently still use Ethereum as the governance, settlement, or anchoring layer.
- One summary explicitly cites reports indicating approximately 58% of current RWA tokenization occurs on Ethereum.
